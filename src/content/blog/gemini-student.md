On August 19, 2026, Google announced that college students could get a full year of its AI subscription for free. The headline is simple. The fine print is not, and it starts with the name itself: what most people are searching for as "Gemini Pro" is actually called **Google AI Pro**, a broader subscription that bundles Gemini with Google One storage, Gmail and Docs integrations, and more. Only some students get that tier for free. Others get quietly downgraded to a cheaper one.

Here's what's actually in the offer, who really qualifies, and what happens when the free year runs out.

## What's Actually Included

The offer splits into two tiers depending on where you live, and the gap between them is bigger than the marketing implies.

If you're outside the US, in other words, you're not getting what the headlines are describing. You're getting Google's mid-tier plan, with half the usage boost and roughly 1/12th the storage. Google AI Pro itself is also only available to US students; the rest of the eligible world (140+ markets) is routed to AI Plus by design, not by mistake.

## Who Actually Qualifies

The eligibility list is longer than "be a student," and each item is a place the offer can quietly fall through:

- **Age 18 or older**, enrolled at a qualifying higher-education institution
- **A personal Google Account**, not a school-issued one. Google said school-issued account support was "rolling out in the coming weeks" as of the August announcement, so if your only Google account is the one your university gave you, you may not be able to redeem yet
- **A valid form of payment on file** at sign-up, even though the year itself costs nothing. No card, no offer
- **Verification through SheerID**, a third-party identity service that checks your enrollment (usually via school email or an uploaded document). SheerID says it doesn't sell student data and purges verification documents after a retention window, but you are handing enrollment proof to a company that isn't Google
- **Not already in specific situations**: family members on a shared Google One plan, Pixel Pass subscribers, and accounts with certain third-party/affiliate bundles are excluded
- **Outside a short exclusion list of countries** for the international AI Plus track: Bolivia, Albania, Canada, Macau, Hong Kong, and Tunisia are named exclusions, alongside the US (which has its own separate AI Pro track instead)

None of these are disqualifying for most students, but any one of them is enough to bounce you out of the flow if you're not paying attention.

## How to Redeem It

1. Go to [gemini.google.com/students](https://gemini.google.com/students) and start the student offer flow.
2. Sign in with a **personal** Google Account (not a school-issued one, for now).
3. Complete **SheerID verification** — typically your school email, or a document upload if that fails.
4. Add a valid payment method if your account doesn't already have one on file.
5. Confirm the subscription. Your 12-month clock starts the day you redeem, not the day Google announced the offer.

The redemption window itself closes **December 31, 2026**. That's the deadline to start the offer, not when your free year ends — redeeming in September gets your free year through roughly September 2027; redeeming in December pushes that out to December 2027. There's no advantage to rushing beyond making sure you redeem before the window closes at all.

## Is It Actually Good for Coding?

This is the part most "how to claim it" posts skip. If you're a student who codes, the honest answer is: it depends on which surface you're using.

The **Gemini app itself** (chat, Gemini Spark, Deep Research) is a solid general-purpose assistant for debugging, explaining concepts, and generating boilerplate, but it's not a coding-specific tool the way Claude Code or Cursor are.

Where it gets more interesting: Google AI Pro is the same umbrella subscription that powers higher usage tiers on [Google Antigravity](/google-antigravity), Google's agent-first coding IDE. A student who claims the free AI Pro year effectively also gets a free year of Antigravity's Pro-tier limits, on top of the free individual tier Antigravity already offers everyone. If you're already using or considering Antigravity, NotebookLM, or Gemini Code Assist, the free year stacks real value on top of the chat access, not just Workspace perks.

If you're outside the US on Google AI Plus, that stacking is weaker. Plus-tier limits are lower across Google's AI surfaces, so the coding upside shrinks along with everything else.

## What Happens When the Year Ends

Nothing dramatic, and that's the point: it's designed to convert you automatically. Unless you cancel first, Google **charges your card the standard price** the day your 12 months end — $19.99/month for AI Pro in the US, a regional equivalent for AI Plus elsewhere.

There's also an annual catch most people miss: you need to **reverify your student status through SheerID roughly every 12 months, for up to 4 consecutive years** to keep renewing the discount. Miss a reverification and you lose the offer, converting to the full price even if you're still enrolled.

Practical takeaway: set a calendar reminder for 11 months out, not 12. That gives you a buffer to either reverify or cancel before the automatic charge hits.

## Gemini vs. Free ChatGPT and Claude

If you're weighing whether this is worth the SheerID hassle at all, here's the honest comparison against what's already free elsewhere:

- **ChatGPT's free tier** currently offers unlimited text chat, with separate daily caps on image generation and web search, plus ads showing up in a sponsored section under responses.
- **Claude's free tier** caps you at roughly 15–40 messages per 5-hour window, restricts you to 2 of Anthropic's 3 model tiers (no Opus), and doesn't include Claude Code.
- **Google AI Pro (free year)** gives you 4x Gemini's usage ceiling, 5 TB of storage, and Gmail/Docs integration that neither competitor bundles for free.

If storage or Google Workspace integration doesn't matter to you, the actual usage-limit upgrade over Gemini's own free tier is the real value here, not a unique win over ChatGPT or Claude specifically. If you're a Google Workspace or Android-heavy student, the bundle math tips further in its favor.

## FAQ

### Is this actually free, or is there a hidden charge?

The 12 months are genuinely free with no upfront charge. The catch is that Google requires a valid payment method on file at sign-up and will auto-charge the standard price the moment the free period ends, unless you cancel first.

### Do international students get the same deal as US students?

No. US students get Google AI Pro (the higher tier). Students in 140+ other markets get Google AI Plus instead, which has half the usage boost and a fraction of the storage. A handful of countries, including Canada, are excluded from even that.

### Can I use my university email to sign up?

Not yet as of the offer's launch — you need a personal Google Account. Google said school-issued account support was coming "in the weeks" after the August 19, 2026 announcement, so check if that's rolled out for your account type before assuming you're blocked.

### What happens if I forget to cancel?

Your card is automatically charged the standard subscription price ($19.99/month for AI Pro in the US) the day your free year ends. Set a reminder before month 11 to review whether you want to keep paying, downgrade, or cancel.

### Is Google AI Pro worth it for programming students specifically?

It's a real bonus if you're already using or curious about Google's coding tools — the same subscription raises usage limits on Google Antigravity, not just the Gemini chat app. It's not a replacement for a dedicated coding assistant like Claude Code, but as a free year with genuine coding-adjacent upside, it's worth claiming even if it's not your primary tool.

## The Bottom Line

The offer is real, and for US students it's a genuinely strong deal: a full year of Google's higher AI tier, 5 TB of storage, and better usage limits, all for signing up and passing a student check. The fine print isn't a scam, it's just easy to miss: international students get a smaller plan than the headlines suggest, a card is required even though the year is free, and the whole thing is built to convert into a $19.99/month subscription automatically if you don't cancel in time. Claim it if you're eligible, but put a reminder on your calendar before you forget you signed up for anything at all.
